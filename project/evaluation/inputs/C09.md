# Recycling Claims with Separate Material Fractions

## Abstract

A sorting process can recover more material while producing a less pure output. We propose to compare manual and optical sorting for a stream containing aluminium and plastic. The study will report recovery and purity separately because the two measures use different denominators [S2]. It will not infer environmental benefits from recovered mass alone.

## Introduction

A single weight can conceal a trade-off between useful material and contaminants. If an output contains more unwanted material, its mass may increase without an improvement in recovery quality [S1]. This proposal therefore treats the identity of the material as part of the measurement. Aluminium and plastic will not be merged into an unspecified total that obscures the sorting task.

## Related Work

The material brief distinguishes recovered mass from purity and warns that mass alone does not establish lifecycle emissions [S1]. The methods brief defines recovery relative to target material in the input, while purity uses recovered output mass [S2]. Those definitions will be fixed before the comparison. Neither percentage will be substituted for the other in the report.

## Methodology

We will compare manual and optical sorting over 20 batches [S2]. Each method will process an equal input mass from each batch. The recovered output will be separated into target material and contaminants before the measures are calculated. We will retain the batch-level records so that a high average does not hide a small number of poorly sorted batches.

## Planned Evaluation

The evaluation will report recovery and purity for both methods, together with their variation across batches. We will examine cases where one method improves recovery while reducing purity. Such cases will be described as a trade-off rather than converted into an overall win through an unplanned weighting rule. Input and output masses will remain available for checking each denominator.

## Limitations and Conclusion

The comparison is limited to the supplied material stream and sorting conditions. It will not establish lifecycle emissions or the environmental effect of processing the recovered material [S1]. A useful result may be a more explicit trade-off rather than a uniformly better sorting method. Keeping the measures separate is necessary for that interpretation.
