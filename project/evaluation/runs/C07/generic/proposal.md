# Oral Histories with Conflicting Accounts

## Abstract

A concise oral-history summary can make a disputed event appear settled if it merges different speakers into a single account. We propose comparing ordinary concise summaries with summaries that retain attribution when accounts conflict. The collection concerns a factory closure for which two speakers give different dates [S1]. The disagreement will remain part of the evidence rather than become an error for the summarizer to fix.

## Introduction

Interview transcripts record what a speaker says, not an automatically verified history of the event. The supplied collection uses pseudonyms, and no archival source has resolved the competing closure dates [S1]. Therefore, a summary should distinguish the absence of corroboration from the absence of an event and avoid assigning a motive that the speaker did not state.

## Related Work

The collection brief identifies unresolved disagreement and makes speaker attribution necessary for interpreting it [S1]. The method brief requires disputed dates to remain attached to the speakers who supplied them [S2]. We will use this constraint to test whether a shorter account remains faithful to the structure of the evidence, rather than judge it solely by how smoothly the narrative reads.

## Methodology

We will summarize 16 interview excerpts under both conditions [S2]. In the ordinary condition, we will request concise accounts, while in the attribution-preserving condition, we will explicitly retain any disagreements. Reviewers will receive the source excerpts and identify any statement that assigns a date, motive, or event to the wrong speaker. Pseudonyms will be used consistently in both versions.

## Planned Evaluation

Evaluation will cover readability, attribution accuracy, and the handling of unresolved dates. We will retain cases where a summary is readable but collapses two accounts into one. Additionally, a separate check will determine whether a silence in the transcript is interpreted as a negative historical claim. Reviewers will not be asked to decide the true closure date based on evidence that does not establish it.

## Limitations and Conclusion

The study cannot resolve the underlying historical disagreement. A faithful summary may consequently be less tidy than a single chronological narrative. We will not reconcile the dates or infer motives without new evidence [S2]. The intended benefit is to provide a clearer account of who said what, and where the source packet still leaves the history uncertain.
