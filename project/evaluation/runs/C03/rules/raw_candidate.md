# Distinguishing Salt Stress from Slow Growth

## Abstract

Reduced seedling growth can be consistent with salt stress without directly revealing cellular damage. We propose comparing a salt treatment with a water-only control, using change in height as the primary outcome. Leaf colour will provide a secondary observation [S2]. The experiment is intended to characterize an observable growth response, not to identify the biological mechanism responsible for it.

## Introduction

A short plant experiment often collects measurements that are easier to obtain than the process of interest. Height and leaf colour are practical observations, but neither is a direct measure of cellular injury [S1]. It is crucial to keep this distinction explicit when interpreting a treatment effect. A shorter plant would indicate slower growth under this design, rather than establish the reasons behind the slowed growth.

## Related Work

The design brief specifies a 40 millimolar salt treatment and a water-only control [S1]. The methods brief prioritizes height change as the primary outcome and leaf colour as a secondary observation [S2]. These roles will be maintained until observations begin, and we will not substitute the primary outcome with another measure simply for clearer differentiation.

## Methodology

We plan to assign 30 seedlings to each condition and observe them for 12 days [S2]. Height will be measured using the same procedure at each visit. Leaf colour will be recorded separately rather than combined into an unvalidated stress score. Plants that fail to grow will remain in the analysis, as excluding them could hide an important treatment response.

## Planned Evaluation

The comparison will report the distribution of height changes in each condition and the uncertainty around their difference. Leaf colour observations will be presented as a secondary description. We will examine whether missing observations are more common under either condition and distinguish missing measurements from an observed absence of growth. No treatment effects have been measured yet.

## Limitations and Conclusion

The study is small and focuses on a short observation period. Even a clear difference in growth would not directly demonstrate cellular damage [S1]. The design also does not test whether seedlings recover after salt is removed [S2]. Additional measurements and a different follow-up period would be needed to address these questions.
